# Behavioral Drill: Technical Failure & Ownership

## Question
"Tell me about a time you made a significant technical mistake or experienced a project failure. How did you handle it, and what was the outcome?"

## STAR Template

### Situation
[Context: The project, your role, and the environment]
I was working on mindfulness and was tasked with determining how to CMS the application.

### Task
[What was supposed to happen? What were you trying to achieve?]
My goal was to build a CMS system for the module, day, activity structure, as well as all of the content. I was trying to make it compatible with the progress service, so that adjustments to the content with an active user base would not be an issue.

### Action
[What went wrong? How did you identify it? Most importantly: How did you take ownership and what steps did you take to fix it?]
The Content Management system became major undertaking. I managed to build it out for the modules, but was unable to continue working through the days, activities and content. I determined I would have spent much more time building this system, than I would personally structuring the content in JSON and uploading it myself. It was not an MVP feature or solution, and while the client liked what I put together for the modules, the whole system ultimately failed. I informed the client about the amount of work this would take, and we determined to gradually upload content as she developed it.

### Result
[What was the impact? What did you learn? How has this influenced your work since then?]
Since there was time spent on this feature that did not ultimately come to fruition, it has helped push me to better prioritize work and improvements that I can propose a confident timeline and solution for. While a CMS system would have been nice, it would have been vanity and ultimately the work that would have been larger than the alternative.

---
*Focus: Demonstrate extreme ownership and technical maturity.*
